This guide shows a simple routine for working with copy-ready AI image prompts. It uses the prompts in this library, but the steps work with any tool that accepts text prompts.

## Copy the whole prompt

Copy the full prompt text, not a shortened version. Prompts in this library describe the subject, light, camera, clothing, mood and composition together, and removing one part changes the look of the whole image.

## Paste it and generate once

Paste the prompt into your AI image tool and generate a first image without changing anything. This gives you a baseline. Results differ between tools and between versions of the same tool, so your first image may not match the example.

## Change one detail at a time

If the result is close but not right, change a single detail and generate again. Try the lighting first, then the camera angle, then the background. Changing three things at once makes it hard to see which change helped.

- Lighting: swap "golden hour" for "overcast daylight" to soften shadows.
- Camera: ask for a wider lens to show more of the scene, or a longer lens to bring the subject closer.
- Mood: replace one feeling word, such as "calm" with "joyful".

## Keep faces and details consistent

If you upload a photo of a real person, only use photos you have the right to use and that the people in them have agreed to. Describe the details you want to keep, such as hair, clothing and expression, in the prompt itself.

## Be clear about what the image is

Images made from a prompt are AI-generated, not photographs. If you publish one, say so where people could otherwise be misled.

## Common problems and simple fixes

- **The faces look different in every image.** Describe the same details each time, such as age range, hair and expression, and keep the rest of the prompt unchanged between attempts.
- **The image is cropped badly.** State the framing in the prompt, for example "full-length" or "head and shoulders", and set the aspect ratio in the tool if it has that option.
- **The colours look flat.** Name the light you want, such as "soft window light" or "warm evening sun", instead of only naming a colour.
- **Text in the image is wrong.** Many tools struggle with lettering. Leave text out of the prompt and add it afterwards in an editor.
- **The result is close but not right.** Save the version you like, then change one phrase and compare the two images side by side.
